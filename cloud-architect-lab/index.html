WELCOME TO THE LAB

Build it. Keep it alive.

One two-minute live round with repeated maintenance: some windows are quiet, others overlap Black Friday, cold caches or DDoS.

  1. Place services. Drag a card onto the canvas or tap its card and then an empty spot. Prices such as $14 mean game credits; your budget is $120, not real AWS spend.
  2. Connect. Click A, then B to connect A → B. Drag to move. Click the source again, empty space or Escape to cancel. Edit nodes and wires while running.
  3. Watch the situation. Green/cyan circles are customers; bright red circles are attacks. The top banner reports current overlaps and recovery.
  4. Hear the soundtrack. Music starts with the round and changes from Steady to Rising, Tense or Critical. Short effects sit above it. Use Music on/off, Volume and Test sound beside Run. The header Sound on / Muted control mutes everything. Preferences are saved.
  5. Recover. Select a failed EC2, Fargate, RDS or ElastiCache and Reboot. Reboot cannot bypass maintenance. Pause freezes recovery and stops music; resume restarts it. Lambda, DynamoDB and Aurora throttle instead.
60% static web traffic needs CloudFront → S3. 40% API traffic needs compute → database. SQS needs a downstream worker; deleting it loses unfinished work. Leave spare capacity for maintenance overlaps.

Original synthesized music and effects run locally, without media downloads or a microphone. If audio is blocked, tap Test sound, check volume and ensure the browser tab/device is not muted. Game limits are illustrative.

 READ THE MODEL, NOT JUST THE SCORE

What this sandbox measures

Request success is completed legitimate work / arrivals. Pending SQS entries are not successes. Deleting SQS fails its backlog immediately; disconnected queues retain it; unfinished work fails at round end.

Grade: A needs ≥98% success, both workload paths, and no modeled single-node path loss in the final design. Otherwise ≥90% = B, ≥75% = C, ≥50% = D, below = F. Earlier failures remain in cumulative totals even after a repair.

Resilience check: removes EC2, Fargate, RDS and ElastiCache tiles individually. Managed-service exemptions do not mean failure-proof. These are reachability checks, not capacity, zone, replication or AWS availability guarantees.

Automatic events: Black Friday is 30–60s (2.4× legitimate traffic); cold caches overlap at 45–60s (15% hit rate); DDoS is 65–90s (90 malicious requests/s); worker maintenance is 95–110s on one fixed EC2/Fargate target. RDS maintenance is 105–115s if an RDS instance is present. Targets never migrate after removal; DynamoDB and Aurora are not scripted maintenance targets.

Shutdown and reboot: more than 105% demand for 5s shuts EC2 down; Fargate 7s, RDS 4s, ElastiCache 6s. Manual reboot takes 6/8/7/5 simulated seconds respectively. Maintenance cannot be bypassed with reboot. Lambda, DynamoDB and Aurora throttle. These are game mechanics, not AWS behavior specifications.

Simulation: deterministic 0.25s steps, fractional requests, equal traffic splitting and a shared capacity ledger. No retry, latency, autoscaling or actual billing model. Moving circles sample measured per-edge attempted rates; they are visual indicators, not individual simulated requests. Reduced motion displays stationary samples.

Persistence: design JSON remains compatible with earlier saves; old mission/traffic fields are ignored. Last 20 summaries stay under cal.rounds.v1, including bounded live-edit and recovery logs. Nothing is uploaded.
